Albert Einstein

A mind that changed our view of the universe.

THE PERSON BEHIND THE EQUATIONS

A life guided by curiosity

Albert Einstein was born in Ulm, Germany, in 1879 and grew up in Munich before continuing his education in Switzerland. He studied mathematics and physics at the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zurich and later worked at the Swiss Patent Office in Bern. The position gave him a stable career while leaving him time to think deeply about the foundations of physics.

Einstein's breakthrough came in 1905, a year that became famous for a remarkable collection of scientific papers. He explained the photoelectric effect, analyzed Brownian motion, introduced special relativity, and established the relationship between mass and energy. These ideas challenged familiar assumptions about space, time, matter, and light.

His work continued to grow in ambition. In 1915, Einstein completed the general theory of relativity, describing gravity through the geometry of spacetime. The theory transformed modern physics and later helped scientists understand stars, black holes, gravitational effects, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

Einstein received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect. After the rise of the Nazi regime in Germany, he settled in the United States and continued his work at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. His legacy reaches beyond individual discoveries: he remains a symbol of intellectual independence, imagination, and the courage to question established ideas.

A JOURNEY OF DISCOVERY

Key moments

  1. 1879

    Born in Ulm

    Albert Einstein was born on March 14 in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg.

  2. 1900

    Graduated in Zurich

    He completed his teaching diploma in mathematics and science at the Swiss Federal Polytechnic.

  3. 1905

    The miracle year

    Four landmark papers addressed the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, special relativity, and mass–energy equivalence.

  4. 1915

    General relativity

    Einstein completed his general theory of relativity, redefining the scientific understanding of gravity.

  5. 1921

    Nobel Prize in Physics

    He received the Nobel Prize for his work on theoretical physics, especially the law of the photoelectric effect.

  6. 1933

    Moved to the United States

    After the Nazi rise to power, Einstein remained in the United States and later worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

  7. 1955

    A lasting legacy

    Einstein died in Princeton on April 18, leaving an enduring influence on physics and scientific culture.

“The important thing is not to stop questioning. Curiosity has its own reason for existing.” — Albert Einstein, as reported by William Miller in LIFE magazine, 1955

WHY HE STILL MATTERS

Question the familiar. Explore the unknown.

Einstein's story is a reminder that major discoveries can begin with a simple willingness to ask a better question. His scientific legacy continues to shape physics, while his curiosity remains an inspiration for anyone who wants to learn, investigate, and see the world from a new angle.
